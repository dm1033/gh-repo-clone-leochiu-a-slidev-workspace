Frame for the day

The Three Roles

DI

The Designated Individual. Owns the organisation’s temporary works procedure and appoints the TWC.

TWC

The Temporary Works Co-ordinator. Owns co-ordination of all temporary works on the project.

TWS

The Temporary Works Supervisor. Assists the TWC with supervision, checking and permits on site.

Exam pointerAll three exist in BS 5975. The TWS does not replace the TWC — they work to the TWC’s appointment.
Frame for the day

The 4Cs

Competence

People who know the limit of their own ability, and when to stop and ask.

Co-ordination

One picture of all temporary works, interfaces and sequence — owned by the TWC.

Co-operation

Designers, suppliers, subcontractors and the site team working to the same brief.

Communication

The brief, the drawings, the residual risks and the permit — actually reaching the people who build it.

Exam pointerThe 4Cs are how failures are avoided. Memorise the four words exactly.
Frame for the day

The 3Ps

People

Competence, pressure, fatigue and the gap between progress and safety. People are fallible.

Process

The BS 5975 procedure: register, brief, design, check, inspect, permit, dismantle. Follow it.

Product

The kit. Product failure is rare unless it is damaged, second-hand, or used for the wrong job.

Exam pointerEvery temporary works failure traces to one or more of the 3Ps not working.
01

What are temporary works?

01 · What are temporary works

A Working Definition

  • An engineered solution used to support or protect a structure so that the permanent works can be built, altered or taken down
  • It also covers plant bases, excavations, access, site security, and works that only exist while construction is happening
  • If it is designed to be temporary, it is still a structure — and it can still kill people
Exam pointer“Describe what is meant by temporary works, and give one example.” Engineered solution + one concrete example.
01 · What are temporary works

BS 5975:2024

Part 1 — Management

Procedures for the control of temporary works. Roles, appointments, register, briefs, checking, permits and records. This is the TWS’s home document.

Part 2 — Falsework

Design and implementation of falsework. Technical rules that sit behind formwork, falsework and many of the site checks you will make.

Exam pointerThe 2024 revision split the old single standard. Procedures live in Part 1; falsework design lives in Part 2.
01 · What are temporary works

The Scope — A to E

  1. ASupporting or protecting an existing structure or the permanent works during construction, modification or demolition
  2. BProviding stability to the permanent structure — propping, shoring, façade retention
  3. CSecuring a site, providing access, or segregating people and vehicles — hoarding, haul roads, fencing, stairs
  4. DSupporting or restraining plant, materials or equipment, including water-borne craft
  5. EEarthworks, excavation support, and temporary works to a watercourse
01 · What are temporary works

The Scope — F to I

  1. FA safe platform for work on land or water — jetty, scaffold, edge protection, towers
  2. GControl of noise, dust, debris, fume, groundwater or discharges — screens, bunds, dewatering
  3. HProtection or support to services, and capacity testing
  4. IFacilitating testing — pressure testing pipes, pile testing, floor load tests, pre-demolition tests
Exam pointerHoardings, piling mats, rebar cages and load tests are all temporary works. If it is engineered and temporary, it belongs on the register.
01 · What are temporary works

Seven Families

On every site

  • Site establishment — hoardings, roads, welfare, fencing
  • Access — scaffold, stairs, edge protection, nets
  • Formwork and falsework — including back-propping
  • Permanent works in a temporary state

Often missed

  • Structural stability — needling, shoring, façade retention
  • Excavations — trenches, cofferdams, dewatering, shafts
  • Construction plant — crane bases, piling mats, hoist ties
01 · What are temporary works

Site Establishment

  • All solid or sheeted hoardings are temporary works — they take wind
  • Heras, gates, flagpoles, cabins and haul roads belong on the register
  • Undesigned hoarding falling onto a pavement is a public fatality waiting
Exam pointerCDM requires welfare and site security. Both are temporary works. Start the register there.
01 · What are temporary works

Ground Is a Structure

  • Do not assume a hard surface is competent — services, poor backfill and voids will overturn plant
  • A piling rig that breaks through a haul road is a temporary works failure, not “bad luck”
  • Any repair to a piling platform must go back to the platform designer
Exam pointer2003, LTS railway: a trench cut through a piling mat, poorly backfilled, rig overturned onto live 25 kV lines. Communication failed.
01 · What are temporary works

Scaffold and TG20:21

  • Work at Height Regulations: calculate strength and stability unless the scaffold is a recognised standard configuration
  • TG20:21 is that recognised configuration — designed to BS EN 12811 and accepted by the HSE
  • Outside TG20 — loading bays, cantilever decks, lifted stairs, ties to weak façades — needs a design
Exam pointerA staircase that will be crane-lifted is not a TG20 standard scaffold. It needs a specific design.
01 · What are temporary works

Standard Solutions

  • BS 5975 allows a “standard solution”: the basic design is already done, presented in a table, with no further structural calculations needed
  • It must state layout, loading, limitations, tolerances, and how to install and remove it
  • Used outside its stated limits, it is no longer a standard solution — it needs a design and a check
Exam pointerA trench box used to the supplier’s tabulated limits is a standard solution. A trench box used deeper, or next to a railway, is not.
01 · What are temporary works

Formwork and Falsework

  • Falsework supports other structures until they can support themselves
  • It is short-term, highly stressed, often built from used kit, and must be destressed under load
  • Top-restrained falsework has no lateral stability of its own — it leans on the permanent works
01 · What are temporary works

What the Pour Does

  • On single-sided shutters, a change of pour height, cement, temperature or rate can jump the concrete pressure
  • Corners and non-square angles concentrate load — they are high-risk details
  • A column shutter braced in one direction only will go over in the other
Exam pointerThe TWS does not redesign the shutter. The TWS stops the pour if the site is outside the brief.
01 · What are temporary works

Plant, Cranes and Mats

High-risk items

  • Tower crane bases and ties
  • Mobile and crawler crane outrigger foundations
  • Hoist and mast-climber ties
  • Piling mats to BRE 470

Why they fail

  • Ground not as assumed
  • Unauthorised excavation through the mat
  • No designer involved in a repair
  • LOLER appointed person and TWC not talking
01 · What are temporary works

Excavations

  • CDM Regulation 22 sits alongside Regulation 19 — all practicable steps, cost is not a defence
  • Services, water and a change of sequence are the usual reasons a trench design is abandoned on site
  • If you cannot build it as drawn, stop and go back to the designer
01 · What are temporary works

Structural Stability

Needling

Carrying an opening. The beam, the packing and the sequence are all temporary works.

Underpinning

Deepening or restoring foundations in short bays. The open bay is the risk.

Façade retention

The structure already exists — old, out of plumb, unknown. Survey and monitor; do not treat it as falsework.

01 · What are temporary works

Permanent Works, Temporarily

  • A wall that is not tied at the top or sides is a sail — wind will take it
  • A rebar cage is a slender structure until it is cast. Frankley WTW, 2012: a 21 m cage collapsed onto two scissor lifts
  • Lifting precast, testing a floor, or pouring against a one-sided shutter are all temporary conditions of the permanent works
Exam pointerPermanent works are “the structure that remains and is used when the project is handed over.” Until then they may still need TW.
01 · What are temporary works

What Failure Looks Like

On the site

  • Collapse of the temporary works
  • Collapse of the permanent works
  • Uncontrolled ingress of spoil or water

Beyond the site

  • Adjacent buildings, rail, highway, utilities
  • Fatalities and serious injuries to the public
  • Prosecution of companies and named individuals
01 · Exercise

Exercise — The Register

  • Identify 20 to 25 temporary works on your chosen project
  • Give each item a design-check category (0, 1, 2 or 3) and an implementation risk (low, medium or high)
  • Say who checks the design, and who inspects on site
  • Every site needs welfare and security — they go on the register first

History, failures and the 4Cs

02 · History

Alexandra Dock, 1909

39

Men killed when trench timbers collapsed at Newport’s South Lock.

46

Buried. Some crushed; others drowned as the tide ran into the trench.

1909

Still the UK’s worst construction accident — and it was temporary works.

0

Warning. The timbering “went without warning” in the afternoon.

02 · History

The Collapses That Wrote the Rules

Cleddau Bridge, 1970

Cantilever construction. Design and sequence. Four dead.

Birling Road, 1971

Birdcage falsework, 1,200 tonnes of wet concrete. One dead, five seriously injured.

Koblenz Rhine, 1971

Falsework. Thirteen dead. Lessons on stability and load.

Loddon Viaduct, 1972

Falsework during the pour. Inadequate bracing. Three dead, ten injured.

02 · History

The Bragg Report, 1975

  • Twenty-seven recommendations, most of them now inside BS 5975
  • Appoint a properly qualified Falsework Co-ordinator — the origin of the TWC
  • All falsework designed; RIDDOR reporting of collapses; trained operatives; periodic inspection
  • Lateral stability in all directions; overall factor of safety not less than 2; design for actual erection tolerances and second-hand equipment
Exam pointerBragg, 1975. The TWC starts here. Factor of safety ? 2. Lateral stability in all directions.
02 · History

From Bragg to 2024

1975Bragg. Appoint a Falsework Co-ordinator. Design everything. Inspect it.
1982First BS 5975. The British Standard Bragg asked for.
1996Revision. Procedure starts to sit beside the technical rules.
2008Falsework Co-ordinator renamed Temporary Works Co-ordinator. Activities listed.
2011Further procedural tightening after more site failures.
2019Single code: procedures plus permissible-stress falsework. About 15% process, 85% technical.
2024Split. Part 1 management procedures. Part 2 falsework design and implementation.
02 · Failures

Why Temporary Works Fail

Before it gets to site

  • No procedure, or a procedure nobody uses
  • No TWC appointed
  • Inadequate investigation of ground, services, adjacent buildings
  • No brief, no design, or no independent check

Once it is on site

  • Site unaware of the design assumptions
  • Unauthorised changes
  • Overloading, or the wrong kit used because it was “what we had”
  • No check of erection; no lateral stability; poor foundations
02 · Failures

Avoiding Failure

  • People — a competent TWC who knows their limit, and a TWS who will stop the job
  • Process — BS 5975 followed, not laminated and ignored
  • Product — the specified kit, within its limits, undamaged
  • The 4Cs hold the three together: competence, co-ordination, co-operation, communication
Exam pointerResearch has put about half of catastrophic events down to temporary works. The 4Cs are the avoidance technique the syllabus asks for.

Who is involved

03 · Who is involved

The Legal Frame

Always in play

  • Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974
  • Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999
  • CDM 2015 — duty holders and Regulation 19
  • Corporate Manslaughter Act 2007 — first case involved temporary works

Often in play

  • Work at Height Regulations 2005
  • LOLER 1998 — appointed person and TWC must talk
  • PUWER 1998
  • BS 5975 is a code of practice, not a statute — the law still bites
03 · Who is involved

CDM Regulation 19

  • All practicable steps to ensure a new or existing structure does not collapse if it may become unstable, or is in a temporary state of weakness
  • Any temporary support must be designed, installed and maintained for foreseeable loads, and used only for the purpose it was designed for
  • A structure must not be so loaded as to render it unsafe to any person
Exam pointer“All practicable steps” is not SFAIRP. Cost is not a factor under Regulations 19 and 22.
03 · Who is involved

Principles of Prevention

First

Avoid the risk. The best temporary works scheme is the one you do not need.

Then

Evaluate what remains. Combat it at source. Adapt the work. Replace the dangerous.

Always

Collective protection before PPE. Give people the instructions they actually need.

03 · Who is involved

The Designated Individual

  • A senior person, usually reporting to the board, who owns the organisation’s temporary works procedure
  • Every organisation involved in temporary works should appoint a DI
  • The DI appoints the TWC, makes TWS available, and checks that firms they employ have an adequate procedure of their own
Exam pointerThe DI is procedural management — not the person signing permits on a Saturday.
03 · Who is involved

TWC and PC TWC

Every project needs a TWC

Competence assessed. Deputy appointed for absence. Scope of the appointment written down. The DI makes the appointment.

The PC TWC

The Principal Contractor’s TWC has overall responsibility for co-ordinating all temporary works on the site — whether built by the PC or by a subcontractor.

Exam pointerPC TWC = overall co-ordination of all TW on the site. That is a frequent exam answer.
03 · Who is involved

The Temporary Works Supervisor

  • Appointed, where required, to assist the TWC with supervision and checking of construction and use against the design
  • Works within a written appointment — including which permits they may sign
  • Stops the work when the site is not as drawn, and tells the TWC
  • Does not become the TWC by being on site when the TWC is not
03 · Who is involved

Visible Leadership

  • Under CDM 2015, principal contractors show leadership through the actions of their managers
  • That means setting standards for temporary works practice — and following them themselves
  • A manager who walks past an unsigned permit has just set the standard
Exam pointerPC visible leadership: setting standards for TW practice and providing an example by following them.
03 · Who is involved

Who Designs It

The cast

  • Permanent Works Designer
  • Temporary Works Designer — in-house, supplier or consultant
  • Principal Designer — co-operate, co-ordinate, pass information

The TWS need

  • A brief that includes residual risks from the PWD
  • Drawings that can be built
  • A named checker, at the right category
Exam pointerThree designer competencies to evidence: skills, knowledge and experience. Attitude, training and behaviours are also accepted.

04

Planning temporary works

04 · Planning

The Control Process

01Identify temporary works at tender. Open the register.
02DI appoints a competent TWC. TWS appointed and recorded.
03Classify each item: design-check category and implementation risk.
04Write the design brief. Include residual risks from the permanent works.
05Design, check to the required category, issue the certificate.
06RAMS and ITP produced, briefed and understood.
07Inspect. Permit to load. Use. Inspect. Permit to strike. Record. Feed back.
04 · Planning

The Temporary Works Register

  • The live picture of every item: description, category, risk, designer, checker, brief date, certificates, permits, inspections
  • Owned by the TWC and kept current through the life of the project — it is not a filing exercise at the end
  • If it is not on the register, it will not be briefed, checked or permitted
Exam pointerThe register is the TWC’s instrument. The TWS helps keep the site columns honest — inspections, permits, as-built conditions.
04 · Planning

The Design Brief

Must include

  • Permanent works drawings and residual risks
  • Site constraints, geology, services, neighbours
  • Sequence, plant, access, and when it comes out
  • Client, rail, highway or licence conditions

Why it matters

  • A thin brief buys a thin design
  • Write it early enough for design, check, approval and procurement
  • The TWC drafts it with the site team — the TWS knows the constraints
04 · Planning

Two Classifications, Not One

Design-check category

How complex is the design? Who is independent enough to check it? Categories 0, 1, 2 and 3. This is not site risk.

Implementation risk

What happens on site if it goes wrong? Low, medium or high. This drives supervision, inspection and who may sign the permit.

Exam pointerA simple design can still be high implementation risk — a standard hoarding over a live pavement. Do not mix the two axes.
04 · Planning

Design Check Categories

Cat 0

Standard solution. No further calculations. Check by another member of the site or design team.

Cat 1

Simple design. Check by another member of the design team.

Cat 2

More complex. Independent checker — same organisation is acceptable if independent of the design.

Cat 3

Complex or unusual. Checker from a different organisation.

04 · Planning

Implementation Risk

Low

Well understood, limited harm if it fails. An authorised TWS may often confirm the installation.

Medium

More to get wrong, and more to hit. Closer supervision. TWC involvement on the permit.

High

Significant harm to people or assets — crane bases, façade retention, deep excavations beside live rail. TWC does not delegate lightly.

04 · Planning

Eliminate, Reduce, Inform, Control

  • Eliminate — the best scheme is no temporary works
  • Reduce — the designer uses skill to cut residual risk
  • Inform — whatever remains is marked on the drawings, not buried in a note
  • Control — the site team, including the TWS, then manages what is left

05

Construction, erection, dismantling

05 · On site

Before Anyone Lifts a Tube

  • Design received, checked and on site — the issue that will actually be built
  • Conditions and materials inspected; damaged or substitute kit rejected
  • RAMS and ITP briefed to the people doing the work, not just signed in a cabin
  • Any deviation already approved — not “we will catch it later”
05 · On site

Permit to Load, Permit to Strike

  • The permit brings the works into use, and takes them out of use
  • It confirms the works are installed as designed, are safe to load or dismantle, and that prior checks are complete
  • Dismantling follows the designer’s sequence — which is not always the reverse of erection
Exam pointerWhen the TWC signs the permit to load, they confirm the works have been installed as required by the design.
05 · On site

Who Confirms Installation

  • The TWC is responsible for confirming that temporary works have been installed as required by the design before loading
  • The TWC may delegate that confirmation to an authorised TWS — typically for lower-risk items, and only within the appointment
  • The TWS does not confirm installation on their own authority, and the designer does not do it by telephone
Exam pointerCorrect answer: TWC, who may delegate to the authorised TWS for low-risk works. Not “TWS without reference to anyone”.
05 · On site

Inspections and the ITP

What the ITP is for

To verify that the works are constructed in accordance with the certified design. Hold points, acceptance criteria, who signs.

Periodic inspections

Required for items that stay in place — hoardings, crane bases, cofferdams, gantries, piling mats. CDM still applies after the permit.

Exam pointerMain purpose of an ITP: make sure the works are constructed in accordance with the certified design.
05 · On site

When the Site Is Not as Drawn

  • Unauthorised change is one of the main causes of failure
  • Stop. Tell the TWC. Go back to the designer. Do not “make it work” with what is on the wagon
  • A repair to a piling mat, a missing tie, a pour that will go higher — all of these reopen the brief
05 · On site

Dismantling

  • You have as much chance of a collapse taking it down as putting it up
  • The method statement must include a dismantling sequence, briefed and followed
  • That sequence is set by the designer — not invented on the last Friday
05 · On site

Records and Feedback

  • Appointments, briefs, certificates, permits, inspections, deviations — kept and maintained
  • Feed back to the permanent works designer and the Principal Designer where the temporary condition taught something
  • If it is not recorded, the organisation will repeat it
